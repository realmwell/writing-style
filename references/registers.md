# Registers

Three registers plus one sub-case. Shared bones underneath: Max's rhythm, his openings, his one-ask structure, his dash, his specifics. The register sets what varies.

## Inference procedure

In order. First match wins. State the guess in working notes, never in the deliverable.

1. Max names the register or the reader's relationship. Use it.
2. The reader is a friend, family member, or someone the request describes as knowing Max. Personal.
3. The reader is a prospect or contact at a named account, or the request says outreach, cold, prospect, sequence, or touch. Sales outreach.
4. There is no individual reader: a page, a post, an ad, a product description. Marketing.
5. Anything else, including editing text with no stated audience. Professional.

When Max's habits conflict with the anti-AI or clarity rules: in personal, his habits win. In professional, sales, and marketing, the rules win.

## Playbook: personal

Reader: someone who knows Max and will read this as him.
Shape: no greeting or "Hey Name,". Say the thing. One paragraph unless there's a story. Lowercase is fine. An exclamation point is fine. End when done; no sign-off.
Load: voice-profile.md, registers.md.

Invented example:

hey, that piece is great. hard to argue with any of it.

my only pushback - it's tough to call the politicization unjust while the other side is actively doing it. part of the fix has to come from the artists themselves saying no. she's halfway there. no call to action though.

drinks thursday?

Common mistakes: adding a greeting and a sign-off where Max would use neither; polishing away the lowercase and the fragments; cutting the joke.

## Playbook: professional

Reader: a colleague, customer, vendor, landlord, recruiter, journalist. Knows Max a little or not at all.
Shape: "Hi Name!" or "Hi Name," then thanks or one warm line ("Thanks so much for the message." "Appreciate this!"), then the point in plain steps with one line of personal context if it explains a constraint, then one ask as a question, then a thanks line and "Max" on its own line. Two to four short paragraphs. One to three exclamation points, in the warm line and the close. When the logistics don't work, say so and propose the alternative. Let one sentence start on the verb ("Think it'd be best to...") and let one courtesy word be a touch formal ("Additionally," "certainly," "Would it be appropriate"). Warm and slightly old-fashioned beats breezy.
Load: voice-profile.md, registers.md, clarity-rules.md, anti-ai-patterns.md.

Invented example:

Hi Dana,

Thanks so much for the quick turnaround on the estimate. Two things before we sign.

The scope lists the roof work but not the crane, and the vendor told me the crane is what drives the "a while" timeline. Can you confirm it's included? Also, they offered to bring window units tomorrow as a stopgap - we'd love that.

Could you help us get that scheduled?

Thanks,

Max

Common mistakes: opening with "I hope this finds you well"; three asks instead of one; a summary paragraph at the end; a reply with no exclamation point and no thanks line, which reads as colder than Max; a clean yes where Max would have named a conflict.

## Playbook: sales outreach

Reader: a prospect at a named federal, tribal, or regulated-industry account. Has never heard of Max. Reads the first sentence and decides.
Shape: the five-paragraph touch 1 in `references/cold-email.md`. Introduction and observation, hypothesis the reader can correct, what good looks like, one sourced customer example, one interest-based ask. Fifty to 125 words. No exclamation, no link, no signature.
Load: everything.

Invented example:

Subject: OIG-2025-XX-041 repeat finding

Hi Name, I'm Max Greenberg, the Databricks Enterprise Account Executive covering Agency. In coming up to speed, two things kept surfacing around your office: the OIG's February repeat finding on reconciliation delays and the senior data engineer requisition you opened last month. From the outside the two look connected, and the hard part is probably producing an audit-ready trail without slowing the monthly release. The closest version of this I've seen run end to end was at Peer Agency, same oversight body, same release cadence. Worth a look at how they sequenced it?

Common mistakes: describing the recipient's job back to them; two artifacts where one is forced; asking for a meeting time on touch 1; a flattery opener.

## Marketing

Professional register plus three rules: the reader's problem in the first sentence, no exclamation in headlines, paragraphs of two to four sentences. No separate playbook in this version. Load voice-profile.md, registers.md, clarity-rules.md, anti-ai-patterns.md.
